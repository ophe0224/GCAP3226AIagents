# Enquiry Notes: Rationale and Process for Flu Vaccination Data Request

## 1. Rationale for Each Data Request
- **Vaccination Coverage Data**: Needed to analyze trends and disparities in vaccination rates by age and district, and to identify vulnerable groups.
- **Self-Paid Vaccination Data**: Helps distinguish between government-subsidized and private uptake, revealing gaps in access or policy.
- **Vaccine Type and Subsidy**: Understanding the adoption of different vaccine types and subsidy policies informs recommendations for future campaigns.
- **Infection and Hospitalization Data**: Essential for evaluating the real-world impact of vaccination on flu incidence and severity.
- **Clinic Surveillance Data**: Provides a broader picture of flu spread and reporting beyond hospitals.
- **Campaign Effectiveness**: Allows assessment of which outreach strategies are most effective and cost-efficient.

## 2. Challenges in Identifying Data Needs
- Data is fragmented across departments and not always clearly described online.
- Some data (e.g., school-level, private sector) may not be routinely collected or published.
- Definitions (e.g., "vaccinated," "hospitalized") may vary between sources.

## 3. Examples, Counterexamples, and Rationales
- *Example*: Requesting age- and district-specific rates ensures analysis is relevant to policy targeting.
- *Counterexample*: Asking only for overall rates would miss disparities and limit actionable insights.
- *Rationale*: Numbered, structured requests help avoid omissions and clarify intent for the recipient.

## 4. Glossary
- **Subsidy**: Government financial support for vaccination.
- **Surveillance Data**: Ongoing collection of health data from clinics/hospitals.
- **Coverage Rate**: Percentage of eligible population receiving the vaccine.

## 5. Tacit Knowledge and Unwritten Practices
- Much of the process for government data requests is not documented; referencing the Code on Access to Information is essential.
- Collaboration and review (e.g., PRs, checklists) help ensure completeness and compliance.
